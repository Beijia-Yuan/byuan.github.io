Research Area One

Spatial and temporal regulation of chromatin organization is essential for cellular functions. Many biological processes requires chromatin sites far away along the contour to come to close proximity physically, such as enhancer-promoter interactions for gene expression regulation or VDJ recombination in immune cells. How do cells achieve this? Do these processes require complex biological mechanisms, or do simple physics laws suffice to explain the phenomenon?

The idea of approaching a fundamental problem with the simpliest model really attracted me. And after analyzing transcriptome data in the previous years, I got curious about the regulations of gene expressions beyond purely data-driven and correlation-based methods. During my undergrad at UCSD, I started to work with Dr. Olga Dudko on formulating chromatin dynamics as a self-avoiding Random Walk. It was initially my senior design project, where I collaborated with Kiana Golden (also a student at Princeton now). But I really liked the project so I stayed one year after graduation to further pursue this.
